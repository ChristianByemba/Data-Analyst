# 6. Act

## Final conclusion

The data shows a clear behavioral split: **members use Cyclistic to commute** (short, frequent,
weekday, rush-hour rides, stable year-round), while **casual riders use it for leisure** (longer
rides, concentrated on weekend afternoons and summer months, no morning commute peak). Converting
casual riders into members therefore means selling membership as a better deal for the riding
*they already do* — leisure and weekend riding — rather than pitching it as a commuting tool they
don't currently need.

## Top 3 recommendations

1. **Pitch membership as a leisure/weekend value plan, not a commuter pass.**
   Casual riders already ride ~63% longer per trip (19.9 vs 12.2 min) and are proportionally far
   more weekend-heavy (37% vs 23% of rides) than members. Marketing messaging and in-app prompts
   should lead with savings for people who ride recreationally and often — e.g. a simple
   cost calculator shown right after a casual ride ("you've spent $X on single-ride/day passes
   this month — an annual membership would have cost $Y") — rather than commute-focused messaging
   that doesn't match how casual riders actually use the service.

2. **Time the campaign to the casual-ridership surge, not evenly across the year.**
   Casual ride volume swings ~14x from January to August, versus only ~4x for members — casual
   usage is heavily concentrated in the warm months. Concentrate acquisition spend and
   conversion prompts in **May–September**, and especially around **Saturdays** (casual riders'
   single busiest day), when the largest number of casual riders are actively using the service
   and most likely to respond to a membership offer.

3. **Target digital media to casual riders' actual usage windows: weekend and weekday
   afternoons, not commute hours.** The hourly pattern shows casual riders have no morning
   rush-hour peak and are most active from midday through early evening. Ads, push
   notifications, and station-based signage aimed at casual-to-member conversion should be
   scheduled for weekend daytime and weekday afternoon windows — when casual riders are actually
   on the app/bikes — instead of the 7–9 AM / 4–6 PM commute windows that work for member-focused
   messaging.

## How the team can apply this

- Marketing (Lily Moreno's team) can use the ride-length and time-of-day findings directly to
  storyboard campaign creative and choose send times for email/push notifications.
- Program question #2 (*why* would casual riders buy a membership) can be tested directly via the
  cost-savings angle in recommendation #1.
- Program question #3 (how digital media can influence casual riders) is partly answered by
  recommendation #3: target casual riders' actual active hours/days/seasons rather than generic
  always-on spend.

## Next steps / additional data

- **A/B test** the cost-savings prompt (recommendation #1) with a subset of casual riders before
  a full rollout, to confirm it actually lifts conversion.
- **Station-level data** (which stations casual riders start/end from most) would let the team
  target station signage and local promotions geographically — not available from this dataset's
  scope but worth adding in a follow-up analysis.
- **Post-ride survey data** (ride purpose: commute vs. leisure vs. tourism) would let the team
  validate the commute/leisure interpretation directly, rather than inferring it from timing
  patterns alone — this dataset has no PII and no purpose field, so this is inferred, not
  confirmed, behavior.
- Revisit this analysis next year once conversion campaigns are live, to measure whether the
  targeted approach actually shifted the member/casual ratio (currently 64.5% / 35.5%).
