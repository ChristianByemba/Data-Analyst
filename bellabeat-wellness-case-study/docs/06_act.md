# 6. Act

## Final conclusion

The data challenges the default framing for a fitness-tracker analysis. The obvious story would
be "encourage users to take more steps." The actual data says something more specific and more
useful: **sedentary time is what tracks most closely with a meaningful wellness outcome (sleep),
not step count.** Sedentary minutes correlate with poor sleep at **r = -0.60**, more than three
times as strongly as steps do (**r = -0.19**). Meanwhile, most of this sample is already
under-active by conventional standards (80% average under 10,000 steps/day, 82% of the tracked
day is sedentary) — so a step-count pitch alone would be preaching to an audience that already
knows it isn't hitting the target, without addressing what the data shows actually matters.

## Top content recommendations

1. **Lead marketing content with "interrupt sitting," not "hit your step goal."** Steps vs. sleep
   is a weak relationship in this data; sedentary time vs. sleep is a strong one. Content and
   in-app prompts for Bellabeat's always-worn products (**Leaf**, **Time**) should center on
   gentle move-reminders that break up long sedentary stretches, positioned as a sleep-quality
   lever — not a steps leaderboard, which doesn't fit an always-worn wellness device as naturally
   as it does a dedicated fitness tracker.

2. **Turn the sedentary→sleep link into a personalized membership feature.** Bellabeat's
   subscription membership already promises personalized guidance. A concrete, sellable feature
   this analysis supports directly: show each user *their own* sedentary-time-vs-sleep-quality
   pattern (chart 4 in the Share phase) inside the app, so the insight is felt as personal
   evidence, not a generic tip — a strong hook for converting free-app users into members.

3. **Schedule engagement content for the early evening, not the morning.** Activity in this
   sample is lowest overnight, rises through the day, and **peaks at 7 PM** with no sharp morning
   spike. Push notifications, content drops, and membership-guidance prompts timed to the early
   evening window are more likely to land while users are actually engaged with their device than
   a traditional "good morning" push.

## How the team can apply this

- Marketing can prototype the "interrupt sitting" messaging angle in upcoming Leaf/Time campaign
  creative and A/B test it against the conventional step-count framing.
- Product can scope the personalized sedentary/sleep insight (recommendation #2) as a membership
  feature, since the data-supporting logic is already validated here.
- Growth/lifecycle marketing can shift push-notification scheduling toward the 6–8 PM window as a
  first test, measuring open/engagement rate against the current schedule.

## Next steps / additional data

- **This dataset has no gender field**, a real limitation for a company whose products are
  marketed specifically to women — before committing budget to these recommendations, validate
  the sedentary/sleep relationship against Bellabeat's own first-party app data (opt-in, already
  anonymized) once enough usage history exists.
- **Only 24 of 35 users logged sleep and only 8 logged weight** — a larger, more complete sample
  (ideally Bellabeat's own users) would strengthen confidence in the sleep and weight findings
  specifically.
- **This data is from 2016** — smart-device adoption and usage patterns have likely shifted;
  treat these as directional hypotheses to test, not settled facts.
- A natural follow-up analysis: repeat this same sedentary/sleep correlation check on Bellabeat's
  own app data once available, to confirm the relationship holds for their actual customer base.
