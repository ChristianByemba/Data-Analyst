# 6. Act

## Final conclusion

Avocado demand in this data is **seasonal, regionally uneven, and price-sensitive** — three
concrete levers a retail pricing and purchasing strategy can act on directly — and the price
history contains a real historical supply-shock event (2017) worth planning contingencies around.
A flat, national, always-the-same-price approach leaves demonstrable margin and volume
opportunity on the table.

## Top recommendations

1. **Adopt regional pricing rather than a national flat rate.** Conventional avocado prices vary
   55% across the 8 major regions ($0.87 in SouthCentral vs. $1.34 in the Northeast) — a
   consistent, multi-year pattern, not noise. A single national price is very likely
   underpricing in high-willingness-to-pay regions (Northeast, Midsouth) and overpricing (or
   losing volume) in low ones (SouthCentral, West).

2. **Build inventory and promotional calendars around the confirmed seasonal cycle.** Prices for
   both conventional and organic peak every year in September/October and bottom out in
   January/February. Purchasing should lock in supply ahead of the autumn peak; marketing should
   plan volume-driving promotions for the January/February trough, when price elasticity
   (r=-0.51 nationally) suggests promotions will move meaningfully more volume.

3. **Treat 2017 as a live case study for supply-shock contingency planning**, not a one-off.
   Prices more than doubled from their 2015-2016 baseline during a real, documented shortage.
   Before the next such event, the client should have pre-negotiated backup sourcing and a
   defined pricing response — rather than reacting in real time.

## How the client can apply this

- Category/pricing team: use the regional price table (`docs/05_share.md`, chart 4) as a starting
  point for a regional pricing pilot in 2-3 stores per region before rolling out nationally.
- Purchasing/inventory: align purchase timing with the September/October peak and the
  January/February trough identified in the seasonality chart.
- Category growth: organic is only 2.8% of volume but grew ~87% (2015-2017, vs. ~11% for
  conventional) at a ~42% price premium — a small shelf-space increase for organic is
  low-risk given its trajectory and margin profile.

## Next steps / additional data

- **This dataset ends in March 2018** — avocado supply, demand, and pricing have moved
  considerably since (organic adoption in particular has likely grown well past 2.8%). Any
  pricing decision should be validated against more recent internal or third-party data before
  being finalized.
- **This is US-wide retail-panel data, not this specific client's own sales data.** The
  regional and seasonal patterns are a strong prior, but the client's own point-of-sale data
  (available internally) should be cross-checked against these findings before setting prices —
  the client's actual customer base and store locations may not match the panel's regional mix.
- **A natural follow-up**: pair this price/volume data with the client's own margin data per
  region to translate "prices vary 55% by region" into a concrete regional pricing
  recommendation with an expected margin impact, not just a price gap.
- **Additional deliverable worth adding**: a simple price-elasticity model (beyond the single
  correlation coefficient used here) fit separately by region, to quantify how much volume a
  given regional price change would likely gain or lose.
