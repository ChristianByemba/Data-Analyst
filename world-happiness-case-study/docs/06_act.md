# 6. Act

## Final conclusion

Across every angle of this analysis — correlation strength, regional comparison, trend, and
top/bottom country comparison — the same two factors stand out consistently: **GDP per capita
and health/life expectancy**. Generosity, despite being one of the report's six official factors,
shows almost no relationship with happiness in this data, and freedom matters meaningfully less
than economic security or social support. For a development-focused client, this reframes where
program investment is best justified by the data.

## Top recommendations

1. **Prioritize economic-security and livelihood programs over generosity- or civic-engagement-
   framed initiatives.** GDP per capita has the strongest correlation with happiness (r=0.79) and
   the largest gap between the happiest and least-happy countries (0.99) of any factor measured.
   Programs that directly improve household income stability are better supported by this data
   than campaigns built around civic generosity or volunteering.

2. **Pair economic programs with healthcare access investment.** Health/life expectancy is the
   second-strongest correlate (r=0.74) and the third-largest top/bottom gap (0.59). A program
   that only addresses income without healthcare access is leaving a large, well-supported lever
   on the table.

3. **Direct the largest share of program budget to Sub-Saharan Africa.** It trails every other
   region by a wide margin (4.29 average vs. 7.27 for the top region) and supplies 6 of the 10
   least-happy countries in 2019. This is the region where the same budget is likely to move the
   needle furthest.

## How the client can apply this

- Program design: weight funding allocation toward economic-security and healthcare-access
  interventions specifically, rather than spreading it evenly across all six factors the report
  tracks.
- Geographic targeting: Sub-Saharan Africa and the lowest-ranked Southern Asia / Middle East–North
  Africa countries are where the data shows the most room to improve, and where the strongest
  factors (GDP, health) are most likely underdeveloped.
- Social-support programs (community networks, family support infrastructure) are the
  third-best-supported lever (r=0.65, gap=0.88) — a reasonable second priority alongside
  healthcare.

## Next steps / additional data

- **This dataset ends in 2019** — before major global disruptions (the COVID-19 pandemic,
  subsequent economic shocks) that plausibly reshaped these relationships. Before committing a
  multi-year program budget, validate these findings against the World Happiness Report's more
  recent editions (2022–2025), which aren't in this Kaggle mirror.
- **Correlation is not causation.** GDP per capita's strong correlation with happiness doesn't
  prove that raising GDP *causes* higher happiness (wealthier countries may simply have more
  resources for the *other* factors too, like healthcare). A program built on this analysis should
  pilot in a small set of countries and measure happiness-score change directly, rather than
  assuming the historical cross-country correlation will hold as a causal lever.
- **Country-level averages hide within-country inequality.** A natural follow-up: pair this
  analysis with income- or region-level data *within* the lowest-ranked countries to target
  programs more precisely than a national average allows.
