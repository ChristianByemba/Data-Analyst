## Key findings

1. **GDP per capita (r=0.79) and health/life expectancy (r=0.74) are the strongest correlates of
   happiness** across the full 5-year panel. **Generosity (r=0.14) is by far the weakest** —
   despite being one of the report's 6 headline factors.
2. **Regional gap is large**: Australia/NZ (7.27) and North America (7.08) lead; Sub-Saharan
   Africa trails at 4.29 — a roughly 3-point gap on the 0–10 scale, the widest regional spread in
   the data.
3. **Global happiness dipped slightly in 2016, then climbed every year through 2019** (5.42 →
   5.41 → 5.42 → 5.45 → 5.50 average), measured on the 146 countries present in every year for a
   fair like-for-like comparison.
4. **8 of the top 10 countries (2019) are Western European**; **6 of the bottom 10 are
   Sub-Saharan African**, joined by conflict-affected states (Afghanistan, Yemen, Syria).
5. **The gap between top-10 and bottom-10 countries is largest for GDP per capita (0.99) and
   social support (0.88), smallest for generosity (0.06)** — the same ranking as the correlation
   analysis, from an independent angle.

## Interpretation

Across every angle of this analysis — correlation, regional averages, and top/bottom country
comparison — **economic security and social support consistently separate happier countries from
less happy ones; generosity and even freedom matter far less than the report's framing might
suggest.** This is a specific, actionable, and slightly counter-intuitive finding for a
development-program client: interventions aimed at economic stability and strengthening social
support networks are better supported by this data than campaigns framed around individual
freedom or generosity.
